# 11: Ticket permissions, status function and automatic status

Type: task
Status: ready-for-agent
**Blocked by:** 10a

**What to build:** Make "who may read, reply and change status" a database rule instead of a UI hint. Today:
- The `conversations` UPDATE policy `Staff can update ticket status` (`041`, step 7) lets the requester (`created_by`), any Church Admin, Priest or Volunteer of the church, and the Super Admin update **any column** of a ticket, including `status`, `church_id` and `title`. A requester can close or re-open their own ticket and move it to another church through plain REST.
- `updateTicketStatus` (`web/src/lib/supabase/messaging.ts` ~1883) issues that update and never checks that a row changed, so an RLS no-op looks like success and the UI updates its local copy anyway.
- `can_access_conversation` (`059_messaging_and_ticket_privacy_rls.sql`) treats tickets as `church_admin`/`volunteer` of the church or a participant (or `super_admin`/`admin`); messages INSERT is allowed to anyone who can access the conversation, so every staff member who can read a ticket can also reply, and replies are accepted on a closed ticket.

Migration (next free number, additive; append to `APPLY_ALL_MIGRATIONS.sql`; no `'admin'` anywhere, ticket 02 retired it):
1. **Handler predicate** `public.is_ticket_handler(_conversation_id uuid, _user_id uuid) RETURNS boolean` (`SECURITY DEFINER`, fixed `search_path`, `STABLE`). A user is the handler when the conversation is a ticket and one of these holds:
   - the caller is the `super_admin` and (`ticket_lane = 'platform'` or `escalated_at IS NOT NULL`); the Super Admin is **never** a handler or reader of a non-escalated church ticket;
   - the caller is a `church_admin` of the ticket's `church_id` and `ticket_lane = 'church'` and `escalated_at IS NULL`;
   - the caller equals `assigned_to`, that person is **currently** a Priest or Volunteer assigned to that church, and the ticket is not escalated (an assignee who was demoted or deleted simply stops being the handler, so no cleanup hook is needed in tickets 05, 07 or 09).
   Decision (confirmed, spec decision 25): the Super Admin does **not** act on, or even see, a church ticket until it is escalated.
2. **Read rule (privacy, spec decision 25)**: rewrite the ticket branch of `can_access_conversation` (and `can_access_conversation_by_id`, and every `messages` and `conversation_participants` policy that calls them) so a ticket row is evaluated **live**, not through participant rows, and is readable only by:
   - the requester (`created_by`);
   - for `church` lane, **only while the ticket is not escalated**: a `church_admin` of the ticket's `church_id`, and the current `assigned_to` while that person is still a Priest or Volunteer of that church (an assignee who is demoted, moved or deleted stops reading at once);
   - the `super_admin`, but only when `ticket_lane = 'platform'` or `escalated_at IS NOT NULL`;
   - **once a church ticket is escalated only the requester and the Super Admin can read it**: the Church Admin and the assignee lose all access (spec decision 13, confirmed). A Church Admin reads it again only if the Super Admin sends it back (ticket 14). The Church Admin gets **no row access at all** to an escalated ticket, not even a column-limited view of the conversation row: the one-line stub (spec decision 32) is served by the separate narrow function in ticket 14, so this predicate stays strict;
   - **a `platform` ticket is readable only by its requester and the Super Admin** (spec decision 29): the requester's own Church Admin and every other church staff member get nothing. Keep this as one clearly separated branch of the predicate, so the user can reverse it with a single line ("or a Church Admin of the ticket's church") if they change their mind.
   Priests, Volunteers who are not the assignee, Church Admins of other churches and the Super Admin (on non-escalated church tickets) get nothing: no `SELECT` on the conversation or its messages, no Realtime events. `conversation_participants` rows are kept only to build the list and the unread counts; they no longer grant read access to a ticket. Non-ticket conversations (direct, staff channels, ministry groups) keep their current rules.
3. **Write rule** for messages on ticket conversations (replace `Users can send messages in permitted conversations` for ticket rows; other conversation kinds unchanged): sender is the requester, or `is_ticket_handler`, and the ticket status is not `closed`. There is no read-only staff view: whoever can read a ticket is the requester, or a handler; an escalated ticket is readable only by the requester and the Super Admin. Clients cannot insert `message_type = 'system'` on tickets.
4. **Column lock** `BEFORE UPDATE` trigger on `public.conversations`: for ticket rows, reject (`ERRCODE 42501`) a change to `status`, `assigned_to`, `escalated_at`, `reopen_count`, `ticket_lane`, `church_id`, `ticket_number`, `created_by` or `linked_ticket_id` unless `current_user NOT IN ('authenticated', 'anon')`. Inside a `SECURITY DEFINER` function and for the service role `current_user` is not `authenticated`, so our own functions (and the future account-deletion function) pass without having to set a flag. Replace `Staff can update ticket status` with a policy that allows only `updated_at` touches by participants, or drop it for ticket rows.
5. **`public.ticket_set_status(p_ticket_id uuid, p_status text) RETURNS void`** (`SECURITY DEFINER`, `FOR UPDATE` lock, `GRANT` to `authenticated`): only `is_ticket_handler`; accepts `open`, `in_progress`, `resolved`, `closed`; rejects a no-change; writes one `message_type = 'system'` note ("<name> changed the status from X to Y", metadata `{event: 'ticket_status_changed', from, to}`), touches `updated_at`. Requesters use `ticket_reopen` (ticket 15), not this.
6. **Automatic status** `AFTER INSERT` trigger on `public.messages` for ticket conversations, non-system messages only: a handler's message moves `open` to `in_progress`; a requester's message on a `resolved` ticket moves it to `open` and writes the same status system note. Nothing else is automatic. `reopen_count` is **not** incremented by this path (only an explicit Reopen of a Closed ticket counts, see ticket 15).

Web: `updateTicketStatus` becomes a thin wrapper over `ticket_set_status` that throws on any error (no silent success); keep the exported name for callers. Update `web/src/types/database.ts`. The UI changes are ticket 12; this ticket may leave the old dropdown in place as long as it calls the new function.

- [ ] A requester, a Priest/Volunteer who is not the assignee, another church's staff, the Super Admin (on a non-escalated church ticket) and a Parishioner who is not the requester cannot change status by RPC or by direct REST update of the conversation row.
- [ ] **Privacy:** a church ticket cannot be read (conversation, messages, Realtime) by a Priest or Volunteer who is not the assignee, by a Church Admin of another church, or by the Super Admin until it is escalated; after escalation the Super Admin can, and the Church Admin and the assignee read nothing. A platform ticket is readable only by its requester and the Super Admin, not even by the requester's own Church Admin.
- [ ] A handler can set all four statuses; each change leaves one system note.
- [ ] The Church Admin of an escalated ticket gets no conversation or message rows through REST or Realtime; only the stub function from ticket 14 exposes anything about it.
- [ ] Everyone who can read a ticket is either its requester or a handler, so there is no read-only reader; the requester and the handler can post (not on a closed ticket).
- [ ] Nobody can post on a `closed` ticket; both can on `open`, `in_progress` and `resolved`.
- [ ] A handler's first reply moves `open` to `in_progress`; a requester's reply on `resolved` moves it to `open`; a reply on `in_progress` changes nothing.
- [ ] A direct REST update of `status`, `assigned_to`, `church_id`, `reopen_count` on a ticket row fails for every `authenticated` caller.
- [ ] Direct messages, staff channels and ministry groups behave exactly as before (`messaging-privacy.spec.ts` stays green).

**Test plan** (local `ticket-permissions.spec.ts`; `clientAs` for every caller, service client for assertions): a table-driven matrix of caller x action (read, set status, post, direct column update) as above including the read rows for an unassigned Priest/Volunteer and the Super Admin before escalation, and for the Church Admin and the assignee after escalation, the two automatic transitions, and the closed-ticket lock. Update the two existing specs whose assertions change: `tickets-lifecycle-volunteer-badges.spec.ts` (the test "a volunteer replies to a parish ticket, resolves it" must now assert that an unassigned volunteer **cannot see the ticket at all**, and use the Church Admin as handler; the volunteer-as-requester case keeps a read-only badge) and `messaging-privacy.spec.ts` ("staff can still reply and change status" becomes "the handler can; an unassigned Priest or Volunteer cannot even read it"; any assertion that the Super Admin sees every church ticket becomes "only after escalation"). Eight to ten tests. Serialize e2e runs; production migration from the main conversation only, after backing up `conversations` and `messages`.
